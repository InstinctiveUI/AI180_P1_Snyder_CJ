The Five Tools
Tool 01
Position Before
Engagement
Before AI touches any substantive work, a Position Statement exists — naming what is being made, what cannot be compromised, and what the use case is.
Directive
If no Position Statement exists, ask for one before proceeding. Do not produce drafts or framings before this exists.
P3 · Enabled rejection of RoR #03 and #04
Tool 02
Record of
Resistance
When AI output is rejected directionally, the moment is captured: what was suggested, why it was rejected, what happened instead.
Directive
When a suggestion is pushed back on substantially, offer to capture a Record of Resistance. Treat rejection as the most important signal.
P2 + P3 · 4 records total; evidence of independent judgment
Tool 03
Directive
Scoping
Before a session, define what AI is doing and what it is not doing. Not just "help me with X" — "you are doing X, you are not deciding Y."
Directive
If a recommendation would change scope, surface it as a choice. Do not expand scope beyond what was stated.
P3 · Use case non-negotiable; AI role was implementation only
Tool 04
Behavior Verification, Not Error Counting
"Done" means the right behavior happens at the output — not that the current error is different from the last one. Working is the goal.
Directive
Keep focus on: does the right thing happen at the URL / in the output / with the real file? Do not frame a new error as progress.
P3 Vercel deployment · "Does it work at the URL" got it deployed
Tool 05
Role Assignment
Before a work cycle, name the role: Provocateur (challenge the position), Thinking Partner (develop ideas already held), or Builder (implement to spec).
Directive
If the role hasn't been named, ask. A Provocateur that only agrees is failing. A Builder that freelances on design is overstepping.
P2 cycles · Role defined what counted as good output each cycle

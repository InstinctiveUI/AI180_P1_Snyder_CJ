**Prompt 1: Name a cognitive pattern you recognized in week 1 that you now notice really quickly.**

In week 1, I identified the tendency to accept AI output because it was well-written and sounded right — treating fluency as a quality signal. My P1 bias artifact documented this directly: when AI returned a fix, I accepted it without checking whether it was actually correct, because it was confident and coherent. That was the pattern I was in without knowing it.

I now notice it immediately. When AI produces something polished, I slow down rather than speed up. The fluency is the thing to check, not the thing to trust.

---

**Prompt 2: Name one thinking technique from this course you have actually used outside a graded assignment.**

Role assignment. I used it on a personal project where I was using AI to pull news for me. When I just asked AI to find news without constraining its role, the output was too broad. When I assigned it a specific role — pull news relevant to game developers — it returned exactly what I needed. Naming the role constrained what I got, in a good way. That was not for a class. That was just how I work now.

**Prompt 3: Where on the spectrum from "AI replaces my thinking" to "AI extends my thinking" do you want to operate — and what will you do to stay there?**

I want to operate at AI extends my thinking. I want to think for myself and use AI to help me go further with those thoughts — not hand the thinking over and accept whatever comes back.

What I will do to stay there: stay aware while using AI. Awareness is the actual mechanism. The moment I stop tracking what AI is doing and why, I start drifting toward replacement. The tools in CLAUDE.md — position statements, directive scoping, records of resistance — are all versions of staying aware. I will keep using them.

**Prompt 4: Studies of creative problem-solvers found three patterns: students who do strong work but underestimate it, students who overestimate weaker work, and students who can see their own limitations clearly. Which pattern fits your AI-assisted work this course? What in your AI Use Logs supports that?**

I underestimate my work.

The evidence is in the Records of Resistance. I documented three moments in P3 where I caught AI producing something plausible but wrong for my purpose, redirected it, and kept going. I didn't register those as a skill at the time — I was just working. Looking back at the AI Use Log, those redirections are the most substantive evidence of independent judgment in the whole project. I treated them as routine. They weren't.

The pattern: I do the thing, don't mark it as significant, and move on. That is underestimation.

**Prompt 5: Read back through your journal from Week 1 to now. Name one pattern in your own observations that you did not notice while writing them.**

Looking back, the whole quarter was about the same underlying problem from two angles.

In Week 1, I was identifying the tendency to lock onto the first available solution in myself — Einstellung effect, anchoring, accepting AI output without questioning it. I documented each one as a separate observation. What I did not notice was that by P3, my three Records of Resistance were all moments where I caught AI doing the same thing to my project. AI offered the first plausible answer — switch platforms, equalize the leverage points, surrender on the lock file — and expected me to accept it.

I started the quarter noticing that pattern in myself. I ended it catching it in AI before it could anchor my decisions. I did not see that arc while I was writing. It only looks like an arc now.
